Study Command Center

Target Exam Date: May 2026

Current Readiness: 60%
Respiratory

Asthma: Assessment & Emergency Care

  • Hallmark Sign: Wheezing.
  • Severe Distress: Difficulty talking (2-3 word sentences), retractions, and "Silent Chest" (absence of wheezing/breath sounds), which is a sign of impending respiratory failure.
  • Peak Flow Meter: Stand/sit upright, inhale deeply, and exhale forcefully and quickly into the device.
NCLEX Priority: During an acute exacerbation, give a nebulized SABA (Albuterol) first. If symptoms don't improve or if paradoxical respirations/silent chest occur, notify the HCP immediately and prepare for intubation.
Integumentary

Common Irritants: Lice, Scabies, & Poison Ivy

  • Lice: Spread by direct contact; treat with Permethrin 1% cream; wash linens in hot water and dry on high heat.
  • Scabies: Look for "burrow tracks" (grayish lines); apply Permethrin 5% to the entire body overnight.
  • Poison Ivy: Immediately wash with cool water; avoid scrubbing or harsh soaps to prevent spreading urushiol oils.
Priority: For bee stings with anaphylaxis, the #1 priority is Epinephrine to vasoconstrict and decrease airway swelling. Always scrape a stinger off (with a credit card) rather than using tweezers to avoid squeezing more venom.
Musculoskeletal

Congenital & Progressive Conditions

  • DDH: Look for asymmetrical gluteal/thigh skin folds; use a Pavlik Harness for infants <6 months to keep legs flexed and thighs abducted.
  • Clubfoot: Serial casting begins shortly after birth; monitor for the "6 Ps" of neurovascular impairment (e.g., pallor, paralysis).
  • Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD): Characterized by Gower sign (using hands to "walk" up legs to stand).
  • Scoliosis: Wear a 100% cotton T-shirt under the brace to protect skin integrity.
Pavlik Harness Care: To prevent skin breakdown, do not apply lotions or powders under straps. Dress the infant in a single diaper and T-shirt under the harness.
